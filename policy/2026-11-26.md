# AI and work: the policy brief
{:.no_toc}

<p class="byline">26 November 2026 · Adrien Joly, London School of Economics · <a href="{{ '/policy/' | relative_url }}">All policy briefs</a></p>

## Today in three lines
- On 23 November 2026 the French Senate voted, in first reading of the 2027 budget, to tax apprentices' pay like that of working students; the measure is not law.
- A survey experiment published on 25 November 2026 finds German managers less willing to use AI instead of hiring a beginner when the work needs judgement.
- The UK alliance set up in June to help firms redesign entry-level jobs has promised a first report this autumn; none has been published yet.

## What governments are doing
### The French Senate votes to end most of the income tax exemption on apprentices' pay
**What it is.** An amendment by Christine Lavarde, for the Senate finance committee, adopted with the government leaving the decision to senators (voted in first reading, 23 November 2026, not in force). Today an apprentice's pay is exempt from income tax up to the annual gross minimum wage, so an apprentice taxed alone pays nothing below 41,424 euros a year. The amendment aligns apprentices with working students, exempt only up to three monthly gross minimum wages, 5,469 euros a year. The same amendment ends the tax exemption on civic service allowances. The Senate votes on the whole budget on 9 December, then a joint committee of both chambers follows; the National Assembly rejected the first part of the budget on 4 November.

**Who it reaches.** Apprentices, and the families whose tax household they belong to. The measure is a budget saving, not designed around AI.

**What the evidence says.** Apprenticeships are one of the main ways young people get a first job, and the review finds AI weighs first on hiring of beginners. Among managers of German firms surveyed from June to August 2026, 31% expect AI to cut their entry-level hiring over two years, and 5.5% expect an increase (Rost and Stephany 2026). No study in the review measures how apprentices' take-home pay affects how many young people start one.

Sources: [French Senate, amendment I-5](https://www.senat.fr/enseance/2026-2027/155/Amdt_I-5.html) · [L'Essentiel de l'Éco, on the 23 November vote](https://lessentieldeleco.fr/9803-les-apprentis-et-leurs-familles-nouvelles-cibles-du-gouvernement)

## What could be done
### Redesigning starter jobs around judgement, not routine
**The problem.** Firms hire fewer beginners where AI can take over the routine tasks they used to learn on. In US data, junior employment at firms that adopted generative AI was about 9% lower six quarters after adoption than at similar firms, with seniors unaffected; users hand the whole task to AI in 52% of exchanges for junior occupations against 40% for senior ones (Hosseini and Lichtinger 2025).

**The option.** Help employers build first jobs around tasks that need judgement and context. In a survey experiment run in the ZEW business survey from June to August 2026, managers randomly told that a job needed judgement were less willing to use AI instead of hiring, by 0.54 points on a five-point scale from an average of 3.21 (Rost and Stephany 2026). In the United Kingdom, a £20 million Early Careers Jobs Alliance, announced on 6 June 2026 and co-chaired by the Prospect union and the government's AI champion for digital and technology, is to give firms practical help to redesign roles while keeping entry routes. It started in digital and technology and promised a first report in the autumn; we found none published.

**What we do not know.** The German answers are stated intentions about a made-up job, not hires, and come from frequent AI users in larger firms. Whether beginners can do judgement work without first learning the routine, and whether redesigned jobs survive cheaper AI, has not been tested. No evaluation of the UK alliance exists.

Sources: [Rost and Stephany (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34099) · [GOV.UK, 6 June 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/entry-level-jobs-support-ai-bootcamps-and-tech-training-as-government-supports-young-people-into-the-jobs-of-the-future)
