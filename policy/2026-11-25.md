# AI and work: the policy brief
{:.no_toc}

<p class="byline">25 November 2026 · Adrien Joly, London School of Economics · <a href="{{ '/policy/' | relative_url }}">All policy briefs</a></p>

## Today in three lines
- On 24 November 2026 the European Commission said early analysis points to weaker job growth among young adults in sectors that use AI more, with no clear sign of net job losses.
- It asked EU countries to pair AI skills with help from the Youth Guarantee.
- A study published on 23 November 2026 finds that large US firms that took up an AI coding assistant cut hiring of junior software engineers by about 40%, without layoffs.

No new release since the last brief. Unemployment was 8.2% in France in the third quarter of 2026 (7.7% a year earlier), 4.6% in the United Kingdom in July to September 2026 (5.0%) and 4.1% in the United States in October 2026. ## What governments are doing
### The European Commission asks EU countries to keep first jobs within reach as AI spreads
**What it is.** Remarks by Executive Vice-President Roxana Mînzatu on 24 November 2026, presenting a Human Capital Recommendation that sets priorities for member states for 2027 to 2028 (announced). She said there is "no conclusive evidence that AI is causing net job destruction overall or in entry-level jobs", but that "preliminary analysis points to weaker employment growth among young adults in sectors with greater AI use", adding that this cannot be put down to AI alone. In a Cedefop survey, 42% of workers reported gaps in using AI tools and 15% had been trained. The Commission asks member states to combine these skills with support through the Youth Guarantee, an existing EU scheme not designed around AI. A European Skills Observatory of jobs and skills data went online the same day.

**Who it reaches.** Young people entering work across the EU, through national Youth Guarantee services.

**What the evidence says.** This matches the review: young hires in the most exposed occupations have fallen in four countries while totals look flat. In France, permanent hires of 21 to 29 year olds fell by 28.3% between November 2022 and March 2026 in the fifth of occupations most exposed to AI, against 7.5% in the least exposed fifth (Bergeaud, Bertheau, et al. 2026).

Source: [European Commission, 24 November 2026](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/statement_26_2572)

## What could be done
### Youth hiring commitments in public contracts
**The problem.** Firms that adopt AI hire fewer juniors rather than lay people off. Among 5,414 large US firms followed from January 2021 to June 2025, those led to adopt an AI coding assistant cut quarterly technical hiring by 29% and hiring of junior software engineers by about 40% within a year, with no change in departures (Raymond and Dillon 2026; one author works at Microsoft, which supplied the data).

**The option.** Make hiring young people a condition of public money. The Milburn review, published by the UK government on 17 November 2026, proposes that youth employment become a required criterion for public contracts above £5 million that create real demand for workers, and that the largest contracts carry explicit youth hiring commitments. It is a recommendation, not a decision, and is not aimed at AI. In France, deputy Matthieu Marchio asked the labour minister in a written question published on 10 March 2026 whether aid to firms in the sectors most exposed to AI could depend on commitments to hire and train young people; no answer is recorded.

**What we do not know.** Whether such commitments create extra first jobs or only move hires between firms. The UK proposal targets defence, energy and infrastructure, not the office jobs where junior hiring fell most. No study in the review evaluates hiring conditions on public money.

Sources: [Milburn review, November 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6afb1fc5ccb3ea58ac8743c1/from-a-welfare-state-to-a-working-state-full-report.pdf) · [National Assembly, written question 13469](https://questions.assemblee-nationale.fr/q17/17-13469QE.htm) · [Raymond and Dillon (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31083)
