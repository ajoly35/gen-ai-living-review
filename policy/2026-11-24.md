# AI and work: the policy brief
{:.no_toc}

<p class="byline">24 November 2026 · Adrien Joly, London School of Economics · <a href="{{ '/policy/' | relative_url }}">All policy briefs</a></p>

## Today in three lines
- On 19 November 2026 France's labour ministry said its hiring aid for employers of apprentices, up to €5,000, is planned to continue in 2027.
- The same day, a senator's written question said the 2027 draft budget cuts payments for that aid by €460.5 million, or 18.7%.
- A US report published in November 2026 finds the share of job ads open to beginners has fallen 3% to 8% where AI use is highest.

## What governments are doing
### France promises to keep apprentice hiring aid in 2027 while its draft budget cuts the funds
**What it is.** A press release from the Ministry of Labour and the minister for vocational training on 19 November 2026 (announced; the 2027 aid depends on the budget law, not yet passed). Aid paid for the first year of an apprentice's contract reaches up to €5,000 for firms with fewer than 250 staff, €2,000 for larger firms and €6,000 for an apprentice with a disability. The ministry says its renewal in 2027 is planned within the limits set by the budget law. The ministry has written to nearly 800,000 employers. The plan was not designed around AI and the release does not mention it. A written question from Senator Valérie Bazin-Malgras, published on 19 November, says the 2027 draft budget cuts payments for this aid by €460.5 million (18.7% less than in 2026) and ends €134 million of regional support for apprentice training centres. The ministry has not yet answered.

**Who it reaches.** Young people looking for an apprenticeship and the firms that take them on. Among apprentices in the final year of a vocational course in 2023-2024 who did not go on studying, 62% held a paid job six months after leaving, the ministry says.

**What the evidence says.** Between November 2022 and March 2026, hires of 21 to 29 year olds on permanent contracts fell by 28.3% in the fifth of occupations most exposed to AI, against 7.5% in the least exposed fifth (Bergeaud, Bertheau, et al. 2026). Part of that fall may be a shift to apprenticeships: their share among 21 to 29 year olds in the most exposed fifth rose from 6% at the end of 2019 to 10% at the end of 2023. If so, apprenticeships are one of the routes by which young people still enter the jobs most exposed to AI.

Sources: [Ministry of Labour, 19 November 2026](https://travail-emploi.gouv.fr/mobilisation-collective-en-faveur-de-lemploi-et-de-linsertion-professionnelle-des-jeunes) · [Senate, written question 10821, 19 November 2026](https://www.senat.fr/questions/base/2026/qSEQ261110821.html)

## What could be done
### Paid public service years as a first job
**The problem.** Firms adjust by hiring fewer people, not by laying them off. Since late 2022, US workers in the jobs most open to AI, in industries that use it, take longer to find work, while job losses barely moved (Ahn and Carollo 2026). A report by RAISE US and the Burning Glass Institute, published in November 2026, finds that in the US industries where AI use is highest, the share of job ads open to beginners has fallen 3% to 8%.

**The option.** A paid year of full-time public interest work as a first job. RAISE US says it is creating "full-time entry-level jobs" through paid service year programmes with partners such as the state of Maryland, in fields such as education and healthcare that it describes as resilient to AI. France already has civic service, which the ministry's campaign promotes; it was not designed around AI.

**What we do not know.** Whether a service year helps young people later get the private sector jobs where hiring has fallen, or only delays their search. Whether schools and hospitals will keep needing beginners as AI spreads. No study in the review evaluates service years where AI is changing hiring.

Source: [RAISE US and Burning Glass Institute, November 2026](https://www.raiseus.ai/news/new-report-white-collar-industries-are-1-5-million-jobs-short-of-trend)
