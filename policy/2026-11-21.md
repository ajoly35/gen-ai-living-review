# AI and work: the policy brief
{:.no_toc}

<p class="byline">21 November 2026 · Adrien Joly, London School of Economics · <a href="{{ '/policy/' | relative_url }}">All policy briefs</a></p>

## Today in three lines
- On 20 November 2026 the UK agreed to send government AI engineers to Liverpool City Region to build tools for young people's careers advisers.
- A Federal Reserve Board staff paper posted on 17 November finds US workers in jobs most open to AI take longer to find work since late 2022, without losing jobs more often.
- First-job seekers are missing from these figures, and most cannot claim unemployment benefits.

## What governments are doing
### The United Kingdom puts government AI engineers into youth job services in Liverpool
**What it is.** On 20 November 2026 the Cabinet Office and the Department for Work and Pensions announced a 12-month partnership (agreed, starting January 2027) between the government's Incubator for AI and the Liverpool City Region Combined Authority. Engineers will build tools that help advisers spot earlier who needs support and connect young people with jobs, apprenticeships and training. Advisers make all decisions. No budget is given.

**Who it reaches.** People aged 16 to 24 out of work, education and training, or at risk of it.

**What the evidence says.** This measure uses AI to deliver help; it was not designed around AI's effects on jobs. The review has no study of such tools, so their effect is unknown.

Sources: [GOV.UK, 20 November 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/governments-top-ai-engineers-to-help-liverpools-young-people-into-work)

### US central bank staff find AI slows hiring rather than causing layoffs
**What it is.** A Federal Reserve Board staff paper by Hie Joo Ahn and Nicholas Carollo, posted by the San Francisco Fed on 17 November 2026 (research, not a Fed decision).

**Who it reaches.** Those who watch US unemployment.

**What the evidence says.** From end-2022 to May 2026, unemployment rose by slightly under 1 percentage point among workers whose jobs are most open to AI in industries that adopt it, against 0.4 points for the least exposed group; the change came through slower job finding, not job loss. A Cleveland Fed study (9 November 2026) found the same pattern: in the most exposed occupations, job searches lengthened from about 10.5 to over 13 weeks. Both class the unemployed by their last job, so first-job seekers are left out.

Sources: [Federal Reserve research, 17 November 2026](https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/board-of-governors/2026/11/artificial-intelligence-labor-market-reallocation/)

## What could be done
### Income support for job seekers with no work history
**The problem.** If AI slows hiring rather than causing layoffs, those trying to get in are hit hardest. In the United States, new entrants usually cannot claim unemployment insurance, lacking a wage history.

**The option.** On 23 October 2026 Senator Ron Wyden released the text of a Stability and Opportunity for Workers Act (no vote or bill number found). It would create a modest weekly allowance for job seekers who do not qualify for regular benefits, such as recent graduates, and add up to 52 weeks of benefits when unemployment rises. The review's evidence points to the same group: in France, permanent hires of 21 to 29 year olds fell by 28.3% between November 2022 and March 2026 in the fifth of occupations most exposed to AI, against 7.5% in the least exposed (Bergeaud, Bertheau, et al. 2026).

**What we do not know.** The allowance's amount and cost, and whether it shortens or lengthens the search for a first job. No study in the review measures first-job seekers' unemployment by exposure to AI.

Sources: [Roll Call, 23 October 2026](https://www.rollcall.com/2026/10/23/wyden-pushes-bills-to-tax-data-centers-aid-displaced-workers) · [Washington Center for Equitable Growth, 23 October 2026](https://equitablegrowth.org/assessing-proposed-legislation-to-expand-unemployment-insurance-to-protect-u-s-workers-amid-ai-related-disruptions/)
