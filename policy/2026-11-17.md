# AI and work: the policy brief
{:.no_toc}

<p class="byline">17 November 2026 · Adrien Joly, London School of Economics · <a href="{{ '/policy/' | relative_url }}">All policy briefs</a></p>

## Today in three lines
- On 17 November 2026, BFMTV titled its morning economics editorial "IA : les premiers emplois menacés" (AI: first jobs under threat).
- Banque de France records show permanent hires of people aged 21 to 29 in the fifth of occupations where AI can do the most tasks fell by about 28% between November 2022 and March 2026, through fewer hires rather than layoffs.
- Of the three measures described here, only the UK's is aimed specifically at AI and entry-level jobs.

## The numbers
{% include employment-table-en.md %}

Youth unemployment in France reached 21.0% in the third quarter of 2026, up from 19.3% a year earlier, while UK unemployment for ages 16 to 24 edged down to 15.1% from 15.3%. In the United States, youth unemployment stood at 9.3% in October 2026, with no October 2025 comparison available, while 40.5% of employed adults used generative AI for work in August 2026. Countries measure unemployment differently, and these market-wide figures cannot show what part, if any, comes from AI.

## What governments are doing
### France relies on a general youth employment plan not designed around AI
**What it is.** On 7 May 2026, labour minister Jean-Pierre Farandou presented "Emploi futur", a 15-measure plan needing neither a new law nor extra budget. It includes "InserScore", an indicator for every initial vocational course showing the share of leavers in work and their median pay.

**Who it reaches.** The plan targets people aged 15 to 24, aiming to raise their employment rate from 34.3% at the end of 2025 to 40% by 2030.

**What the evidence says.** The plan was not built around AI. Yet Banque de France researchers (Palladino, Bergeaud, Bertheau, Bunel and Degos, 8 October 2026) found permanent-contract hires for ages 21 to 29 fell by about 28% from November 2022 to March 2026 in the fifth of occupations where AI can do the most tasks, against 8% where it can do the least, though higher interest rates, remote work and the post-Covid tech correction also hit these jobs.

Sources: [France 24 with AFP, 7 May 2026](https://www.france24.com/fr/info-en-continu/20260507-le-gouvernement-pr%C3%A9sente-une-strat%C3%A9gie-pour-l-emploi-des-jeunes) · [Banque de France, Bloc-notes Éco 463](https://www.banque-france.fr/fr/system/files?file=2026-10%2FBillet_463-VF.pdf)

### The United Kingdom funds entry-level job redesign and AI training pilots
**What it is.** On 6 June 2026, the technology and work departments announced a £20 million Early Careers Jobs Alliance to map how AI changes entry-level jobs and help firms redesign them, sitting alongside the wider £820 million Youth Guarantee.

**Who it reaches.** Free AI bootcamps in Lancashire and Greater Manchester offer a guaranteed paid apprenticeship to pupils at risk of leaving school after GCSEs, while people aged 18 to 24 out of work, education or training in the North East get at least 6 months of hands-on AI job training from early 2027.

**What the evidence says.** Of the three measures described in this issue, this is the only one aimed directly at AI and entry-level work, matching the Banque de France finding that labour market shifts occur through fewer junior hires rather than layoffs.

Source: [GOV.UK, 6 June 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/entry-level-jobs-support-ai-bootcamps-and-tech-training-as-government-supports-young-people-into-the-jobs-of-the-future)

### United States senators have proposed bills focused on layoffs rather than hiring
**What it is.** Senators Mark Warner and Josh Hawley's AI-Related Job Impacts Clarity Act would require large companies and federal agencies to report AI-related layoffs, while Senator Ron Wyden's Stability and Opportunity for Workers Act would fund retraining and extend unemployment benefits from 26 to 52 weeks if unemployment hits 8.5%.

**Who it reaches.** Neither bill had been voted on as of 23 October 2026, per CBS News, and both target workers who lose existing jobs.

**What the evidence says.** Both proposals focus on layoffs, whereas Banque de France records show total employment in heavy AI-using firms has not clearly diverged from other firms; a young person never hired is neither laid off nor eligible for benefits.

Source: [CBS News, 23 October 2026](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/ai-layoffs-congress-bills-unemployment-benefits-tax/)

## What could be done
### Guidance for pupils and students
**The problem.** Pupils and students pick courses using past outcomes, while junior hiring is shifting fastest in some of the occupations those courses feed into.

**The option.** Governments can publish, alongside course outcomes like France's planned InserScore, how hiring of under-30s is moving in the corresponding occupations, using existing administrative records by age and occupation.

**What we do not know.** Measures of what AI can do in an occupation are not forecasts, part of the hiring drop may reverse with the economic cycle, and steering an entire cohort away from a field risks creating future shortages.

### Income for young people who cannot find a first job
**The problem.** Unemployment insurance relies on past work, leaving out young people who cannot find a first job, which is where hiring has slowed.

**The option.** Current proposals include guaranteed paid placements, such as the UK's 55,000 guaranteed jobs for young people in long-term unemployment, alongside longer jobless benefits like the Wyden bill for those with work history.

**What we do not know.** It remains unclear whether the drop in junior hiring is temporary or lasting, which determines whether short placements suffice, and no study yet measures how these schemes perform in occupations where AI is changing hiring.
