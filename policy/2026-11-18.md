# AI and work: the policy brief
{:.no_toc}

<p class="byline">18 November 2026 · Adrien Joly, London School of Economics · <a href="{{ '/policy/' | relative_url }}">All policy briefs</a></p>

## Today in three lines
- On 17 November 2026 the UK government accepted Alan Milburn's call for a guaranteed route into study, training or work for every young person.
- The Milburn report proposes a small body to track where entry-level jobs shrink; the government has not yet answered.
- France Travail's first study on AI: 38% of employers use or test it, 6% think it could compete with people in their main occupation.

## What governments are doing
### The United Kingdom promises a route into work or study for every young person
**What it is.** Alan Milburn's independent review of young people and work was published on 17 November 2026, and the government accepted (announced, not in force) an Opportunity and Support Guarantee, to be backed by law, offering every young person not earning or learning a college place, training, an apprenticeship, work experience or a job through local services from next year. On 14 November the work department said its fully funded six-month jobs reach all of Great Britain from the end of November.

**Who it reaches.** Close to a million people aged 16 to 24 are out of work, education and training. The guaranteed jobs aim to reach more than 90,000 young people on benefits by March 2029; from April 2027 they qualify after 12 months out of work, not 18.

**What the evidence says.** These measures were not designed around AI; the report says it would be wrong to blame AI for the rise, but warns that entry-level tasks may disappear faster than new routes are created. In French records, permanent hires of 21 to 29 year olds fell by 28.3% from November 2022 to March 2026 in the fifth of occupations most exposed to AI, against 7.5% in the least exposed (Bergeaud, Bertheau, et al. 2026).

Sources: [GOV.UK, 17 November 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/government-commits-to-new-settlement-for-young-people-and-to-invest-in-success-rather-than-pay-for-failure) · [Milburn report](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6afb1fc5ccb3ea58ac8743c1/from-a-welfare-state-to-a-working-state-full-report.pdf) · [GOV.UK, 14 November 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/90000-young-people-furthest-from-work-to-be-given-guaranteed-job-no-matter-where-they-live)

### France's public employment service publishes its first study on AI and jobs
**What it is.** On 17 November 2026 France Travail launched an Observatory and published its first study, a survey of 16,000 employers: 38% use or test AI, 14% use it in hiring, and 6% think it could compete with people in their main occupation within five years. Among users, 70% expect staff to use generative AI; 22% have trained them.

**Who it reaches.** Job seekers and their advisers; it gives no figures on young people.

**What the evidence says.** Few employers expect replacement, yet French records show young hires already falling in exposed occupations.

Sources: [France Travail, Observatory](https://www.francetravail.org/accueil/actualites/2026/l-observatoire-france-travail-anticiper-l-avenir-du-travail-pour-mieux-agir.html?type=article) · [France Travail, study](https://www.francetravail.org/accueil/actualites/infographies/2026/l-impact-de-l-ia-a-5-ans-sur-les-metiers-la-vision-des-employeurs.html?type=article)

## What could be done
### Tracking where first jobs are shrinking
**The problem.** Official figures cover all occupations, while AI appears to change young people's hiring first.

**The option.** The Milburn report proposes a small AI Youth Observatory publishing each year where entry routes shrink, by sector and place, plus retraining pilots in sectors such as call centres. In US data on 281,000 firms, those adopting generative AI had about 9% fewer junior employees six quarters later than other firms, through fewer hires, with senior jobs unaffected (Hosseini and Lichtinger 2025).

**What we do not know.** Losses inside existing firms may show up before jobs created by new firms (Esposito and Zhang 2026), so early counts may overstate the damage.
