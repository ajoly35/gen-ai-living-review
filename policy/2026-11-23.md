# AI and work: the policy brief
{:.no_toc}

<p class="byline">23 November 2026 · Adrien Joly, London School of Economics · <a href="{{ '/policy/' | relative_url }}">All policy briefs</a></p>

## Today in three lines
- On 18 November 2026 a US House committee advanced the AWARE Act, which would make federal statisticians measure AI use at work.
- On 20 November Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook said younger adults face longer job searches, which some link to AI.
- A study posted on 20 November finds US firms most exposed to AI agents cut junior job ads, not senior ones.

## What governments are doing
### A US House committee advances a bill to measure AI use at work
**What it is.** The AWARE Act (H.R. 9381), introduced by Representative Tim Walberg on 22 June 2026, was reported with amendments by the Committee on Education and Workforce on 18 November 2026 (committee stage passed, no House vote yet). It would require the Bureau of Labor Statistics to collect statistics on AI use at work, starting within 18 months of becoming law. Its findings say current surveys cannot measure AI's effects on hiring, wages or employment in time.

**Who it reaches.** Anyone who uses US job figures. The text does not mention age or first jobs.

**What the evidence says.** Measures of which jobs AI could do do not separate AI replacing workers from AI helping them (Imas and Schaal 2026), and use lags theory: computer and mathematical jobs are 94% exposed in theory but 33% in observed use (Massenkoff and McCrory 2026).

Source: [H.R. 9381 as reported, 18 November 2026](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119hr9381rh/html/BILLS-119hr9381rh.htm)

### A US central bank governor points to young people's job searches
**What it is.** A speech by Governor Lisa Cook in Santiago, Chile, on 20 November 2026 (a speech, not a Fed decision). She said "younger adults are experiencing prolonged job searches", which some attribute to "nascent pressure from AI on entry-level positions", and that the "low hire" environment is hard for new entrants, while AI leaves little mark on overall unemployment.

**Who it reaches.** Those who follow US interest rate decisions.

**What the evidence says.** It matches Federal Reserve Board staff work (Ahn and Carollo 2026): since late 2022, workers in the jobs most open to AI take longer to find work, without losing jobs more often.

Source: [Federal Reserve, 20 November 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/cook20261120a.htm)

## What could be done
### Paying firms to hire and train beginners
**The problem.** Beginners have long paid for their training by doing routine work; if AI does that work, firms may cut training short (Garicano and Rayo 2025). Among listed US firms, the more exposed a firm was to AI agents, the more its job ads slowed after agents improved in September 2025: 4.73% lower growth over six months for a typical gap in exposure, with junior ads down 4.54% and no significant change for senior ones (Borri et al. 2026).

**The option.** The UK pays a £3,000 Youth Jobs Grant to businesses hiring eligible young unemployed people and £2,000 to small employers in England taking on young apprentices. Announced in spring 2026, the funding was confirmed in the Budget of 28 October 2026. It was not designed around AI. Governor Cook said firms "may need to take innovative and creative steps to train their future workforce". In France, apprentices' share among 21 to 29 year olds in the most exposed fifth of occupations rose from 6% at the end of 2019 to 10% at the end of 2023 (Bergeaud, Bertheau, et al. 2026).

**What we do not know.** How many funded hires would have happened anyway, and whether a grant can offset a lasting cut in junior work. No study in the review evaluates hiring grants where AI is changing hiring.

Source: [HM Treasury, Budget 2026, 28 October 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6ae20b9039d29f3f9b9b3f55/E03668986_HMT_Budget_2026_TEXT_PRINT__1_.pdf)
